Method

Tag the parents.
Every offspring is marked.

Parentage-based tagging turns a hatchery's broodstock genotypes into a heritable tag. Genotype each spawned adult once, and any of their offspring sampled later — as a smolt in a trap, a returning adult on the spawning grounds, or a fish caught in Bellingham Bay — can be traced back to its parents, its hatchery, its brood year and its release group from a tiny fin clip.

  1. Sample broodstock

    Every adult spawned at the hatchery (e.g., Kendall Creek or Skookum Creek) gives a small fin-clip tissue sample, and spawning crosses are recorded.

  2. Genotype parents

    DNA is genotyped at a fixed of a few hundred loci, often by . The parent genotypes become the tag database.

  3. Release offspring

    Juveniles are released on-station or from . No physical tag is needed — the whole cohort carries the mark in its DNA.

  4. Sample in the field

    Fin clips are collected from smolt traps, spawning-ground carcass surveys, broodstock returns and fisheries across the basin.

  5. Assign parentage

    Each unknown fish is compared with every candidate parent pair. A true shows no beyond genotyping error.

  6. Read the tag

    An assignment reveals origin, brood year, age and release group. Fish that assign to no parent pair are presumed natural-origin (or from an untagged source).

Why PBT on the Nooksack

  • The basin holds two populations — North/Middle Fork and South Fork — within the ESA-listed .
  • Recovery hatchery fish are often left unclipped, so an can't separate them from natural fish. PBT can.
  • Knowing and between forks guides broodstock and release decisions.
  • Terminal and in Bellingham Bay can be apportioned by program and brood year.
